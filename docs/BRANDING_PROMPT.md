# Branding brief for Gemini

Copy the following prompt into Gemini. It requests naming and logo concepts without selecting or changing the application's identity.

```text
Act as a senior brand strategist and Android app icon designer. Develop a memorable name and an original logo for this app.

Product:
A native Android task manager built with Kotlin and Jetpack Compose. It works offline, has no ads or analytics, and needs no Google account or Play Services. Optional CalDAV synchronization connects to a server chosen by the user. Users control whether syncing happens while using the app, only while charging, or manually. Features include lists, a chronological task summary, recurring tasks, reminders, subtasks, tags, priorities, custom drag ordering, light/dark themes, and a home-screen widget. It is intended for GitHub and eventual F-Droid distribution.

Brand character:
Independent, clear, dependable, approachable, and calm. The name and icon should be distinctive enough to recognize among many apps, while remaining simple and professional.

First, propose names:
- Provide 12 strong names, preferably 4–8 letters and one or two syllables.
- Include pronunciation, the intended association, and one sentence explaining memorability.
- Prefer names that are easy to say, spell, and recall after hearing them once.
- Avoid generic combinations such as TaskMaster, TodoPro, SmartTasks, or a name built around Google, Android, CalyxOS, or DAV.
- Rank the strongest three and explain their trade-offs.
- If you can browse, check for obvious existing task-app and software-name conflicts. Distinguish search findings from assumptions; do not claim a name or domain is available without evidence.

Then develop logo concepts:
- For each of your top three names, propose one visually distinct logo direction.
- Create a comparison sheet showing those three icons at equal size, clearly labeled outside the icons.
- Use an original, simple silhouette that remains recognizable at 24, 48, and 96 pixels.
- A subtle reference to order, a daily rhythm, progress, or bringing tasks together is welcome. Avoid a generic clipboard or a plain checkmark inside a blue circle.
- Do not imitate Google Tasks, Todoist, TickTick, Microsoft To Do, or their color arrangements.
- Keep text, initials that require explanation, fine lines, small details, heavy shadows, and complex gradients out of the icon.
- Use a restrained palette with one primary color and, if useful, one accent. Show both light and dark backgrounds and a monochrome version.
- Keep the essential symbol within the central safe area so it survives Android adaptive icon masks. Show circle, rounded-square, and squircle previews.

Deliver the comparison first; wait for my choice before finalizing an identity. After I choose, provide a clean 1024×1024 master, a 512×512 store icon, a transparent symbol, a monochrome symbol, HEX colors, and vector/SVG geometry if your tools support it. Keep the Android foreground and background separable. Clearly identify any formats you cannot actually export.

Choose quality over quantity: if a name or visual concept feels interchangeable with dozens of productivity apps, replace it before presenting it.
```
